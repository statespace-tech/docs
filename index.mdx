Statespace runs a commit of your app on a small share of real traffic. A control runs beside it. Statespace records what happens and puts the evidence on the commit, as a commit status.

A person or a coding agent adds the commit as an **arm**. The proxy in front of your app sends 5% of the visitors to the arm and 5% to the control. All other visitors get your live service, as before.

<TrafficDiagram />

Your CI builds the images. Statespace runs only images of builds that your CI recorded. Your settings, events, and metrics are in `statespace.toml`, in your repo.

## How you use it

1. Your CI builds each push and records the build with `statespace-tech/action@v1`.
2. `ssp proxy` runs in front of your app.
3. You add a commit as an arm with `ssp arm add a1b2c3d --app shop-prod`.
4. The commit status `statespace/shop-prod` shows the evidence: subjects, the split check, your headline metric, and errors.
5. You remove the arm with `ssp arm remove`, or stop it at once with `ssp arm stop`.

Statespace shows numbers for each side. It does not tell you which side is better. You and your agents decide.

## Results

Statespace shows the results of one version of an experiment, with one column for each arm. It shows no difference between the arms, and it does not pick a winner.

```text
checkout v1 · shop-prod · since 2026-11-04 09:12 UTC · 2,392 subjects · split ok

latency (ms)                 c0ffee1                   a1b2c3d
  p50                        41                        44
  p95                        180                       205
  p99                        420                       610

events (estimate [95% CI])   c0ffee1                   a1b2c3d
  requests_4xx               0.1121 [0.0950, 0.1318]   0.1178 [0.1003, 0.1378]
  requests_5xx               0.0274 [0.0193, 0.0388]   0.0438 [0.0331, 0.0578]
  signup                     0.0208 [0.0136, 0.0306]   0.0261 [0.0184, 0.0368]
  search                     0.2804 [0.2554, 0.3068]   0.2753 [0.2499, 0.3007]

Find it in your own data tools: statespace.arm = "c0ffee1…" | "a1b2c3d…"
```

The first line has the experiment and its version, the app, the time of the version, the subjects of all arms, and the [split check](/concepts/events#the-split-check): `split ok` or `split off`.

| Section | Row | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `latency (ms)` | `p50`, `p95`, `p99` | The 50th, 95th, and 99th percentiles of the time to the response headers, in whole milliseconds |
| `events (estimate [95% CI])` | `requests_4xx` | The share of the subjects with one or more responses with a status from 400 to 499 |
| | `requests_5xx` | The share of the subjects with one or more responses with a status from 500 to 599 |
| | `<event>` | The share of the subjects with one or more matches of the event. One row for each event, in the order of `statespace.toml` |

- A share is a fraction from 0 to 1, with 4 decimals. `0.0261` is 2.61% of the subjects of the arm.
- `[0.0184, 0.0368]` is the 95% confidence interval (CI) of the share: a Wilson interval.
- An arm with fewer than 30 subjects shows `[–]` for its intervals, such as `0.0261 [–]`.
- A value without data, such as the latency of an arm with no requests, shows `–`.
- The latency comes from 17 fixed ranges of time. Above 10000 ms, a percentile shows `10000`.
- Live and overrides are never in the results. A subject whose arm was not ready counts only for the split check.
- The experiment page of the dashboard shows the same two sections, with the same names.

When a version is stopped, the first line shows its start and stop:

```text
checkout v1 · shop-prod · 2026-11-04 09:12 – 2026-11-05 10:00 UTC · 2,392 subjects · split ok
```

## Versions

Each start begins a new version. Results are always of one version. So results never mix two setups.

- `ssp experiment show checkout` shows the current or last version.
- `ssp experiment show checkout --version 2` shows version 2.
- The experiment page of the dashboard has a version picker.

When a version stops, Statespace keeps its final results as a snapshot. A stopped version always shows its snapshot.

## Evidence on the commit

Statespace posts a commit status on the commit of each arm. There is one status for each app and experiment. Its name is `statespace/<app>/<experiment>`, such as `statespace/shop-prod/checkout`. Your CI does nothing for this.

```text
statespace/shop-prod/checkout  pending
v4 running · 0.05 · signup 0.0261 [0.0184, 0.0368]
· 1,188 subjects · split OK
```

The running line has:

1. the version;
2. the share of the arm;
3. the headline: the first event, as a share of the subjects of the arm, with its 95% interval;
4. the subjects of the arm;
5. the split check, from 100 subjects.

Each line has the numbers of its own arm only. When the experiment has no events, the headline is `requests_5xx`, such as `requests_5xx 0.0438 [0.0331, 0.0578]`.

### When Statespace posts

- At each change of state: start, stop, removal, and an arm that is not ready.
- Every hour while the experiment runs, when the description changed.
- Only for arms of running experiments, and one last time at stop or removal. Old lines stay as they were last written.

GitHub accepts 1,000 statuses for each commit and name at most. Statespace stops the hourly posts at 950, to keep room for changes of state.

### The state says where the arm is

The state of the status says where the arm is. It never says that the arm is good or bad. It is never `failure`.

| The arm | State |
| --- | --- |
| Is in a running experiment | `pending` |
| Is in a stopped experiment, or is removed | `success` |

See [Commit status](/reference/commit-status) for each description.

## More evidence

- **Details** on the status opens the experiment in the dashboard.
- `ssp experiment show <name>` shows the results, where to find the version in your own data tools, the override links while the version runs, and the versions.
- `ssp experiment show <name> --json` gives the same data to agents, as JSON.
- Your own data tools keep the detail of each request, with the arm as a label. See [Find it in your own data tools](/guides/own-tools).

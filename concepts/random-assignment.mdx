---
title: Random assignment
description: Keep each subject in one variant, and each experiment's subjects apart.
---

Random assignment gives each eligible subject one variant. Each published experiment version records its variants, their allocation ranges, and its share of the app's traffic.

## Stable identity

Choose an identifier at the level where treatment can vary independently. The experiment's subject rule is a CEL expression that reads it from the request.

```shell
ssp experiment create rank-v2 --app atlas --variant atlas-search=0.5 \
  --subject 'request.headers["x-user-id"]'
```

Without `--subject`, the subject is `request.cookies.ssp_sid`. `ssp host` sets that cookie for every new visitor, so a browser keeps its variant. A request whose rule yields no subject gets control and is not recorded.

The subject rule cannot change after the experiment starts. Changing it would mix two populations.

## Stable bucket

`ssp host` calculates a bucket from a salt and the subject ID.

```text
bucket = first 53 bits of SHA-256(salt + ":" + subject_id) / 2^53
```

The same subject, salt, and allocation ranges produce the same variant on every host and every request.

## Traffic slices

Running experiments of an app never share a subject. Each running experiment holds a slice of the app's buckets, calculated with the app's salt. `--traffic` sets the slice's size.

```shell
ssp experiment create rank-v2 --app atlas --variant atlas-search=0.5 --traffic 0.2
```

A subject outside every slice gets control and belongs to no experiment. `ssp experiment start` fails when the app's free traffic is smaller than the slice. A restarted experiment keeps its previous slice when it is still free.

## Variant allocation

Within its slice, an experiment calculates a second bucket with its own salt. Each variant has explicit ranges of it. The remaining interval belongs to control. For an experiment with one 50% variant, the allocation is shown below.

```text
atlas-search: [0.0, 0.5)
control:      [0.5, 1.0)
```

## New versions

An experiment keeps one salt for its lifetime. Starting a changed draft keeps each variant's existing ranges where its new weight permits. New or expanded variants use free ranges. Publishing a new version of a variant keeps its ranges, so its subjects stay in the variant.

Weight changes can still move subjects between variants. Analyze each experiment version separately. Starting a new version stops assignments to the previous one.

## Unavailable variants

A variant that is starting, failing, or over a host's `--max-variants` cannot serve its subjects. They get control, and their runs record `reason = 'variant-unavailable'` with the variant they were assigned. Exclude these subjects, or analyze them by assignment, as your estimand requires.

## Forced assignment

Statespace does not force a subject into a variant in production. Keep manual overrides out of a randomized comparison. `ssp host --local` chooses a variant per request with the `x-statespace-variant` header, for testing only.

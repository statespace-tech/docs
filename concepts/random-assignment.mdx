---
title: Random assignment
description: How each subject keeps one arm across requests and proxies, and how experiments of an app stay apart.
---

How does a subject get its arm, and keep it? Each experiment version records its arms, their commits, their shares, and their ranges of buckets. The proxy maps each subject to a bucket, and the bucket to an arm.

## Stable identity

The subject is the unit of randomization. Choose an identifier at the level where the treatment can vary independently. The `subject` rule is a CEL expression that reads it from the request.

```toml statespace.toml
subject = 'request.headers["x-user-id"]'
```

Without a `subject` rule, the subject is `request.cookies.ssp_sid`. `ssp proxy` sets that cookie for each new visitor, so a browser keeps its arm. A request whose rule gives no subject gets the default, and is not recorded.

All arms of an experiment use the same subject rule. A commit with another rule does not run in that experiment.

## Stable bucket

`ssp proxy` calculates a bucket from the app's salt and the subject ID.

```text
hash   = SHA-256(salt + ":" + subject_id)
bucket = first 53 bits of hash / 2^53
```

The same subject, salt, and ranges give the same arm on every proxy and every request.

## Arm allocation

Each arm and control holds ranges of the app's buckets. The size of its ranges is its share. For an arm and its control at 10% each, the allocation can look like this:

```text
recent-clicks: [0.0, 0.1)
control:       [0.1, 0.2)
default:       every other bucket
```

Running experiments of an app never share a subject, because their ranges never overlap. A subject outside every range gets the default, and is in no experiment. The app's traffic limit caps the total; an arm that does not fit waits, first come, first served.

## New versions

A new commit of an arm, or a new arm in the experiment, starts a new version. An arm keeps its ranges across versions, so the subjects of the other arms stay where they are.

- A control of a new base takes the ranges of a control that went.
- An arm whose share grows takes free ranges.
- An arm whose share shrinks keeps the start of its ranges.

Share changes can still move subjects between arms, and a new version stops assignment to the previous one. So analyze each experiment version separately.

## Unavailable arms

An arm that is starting, failing, or over a proxy's `--max-arms` cannot serve its subjects. They get the default, and their runs record `reason = 'arm-unavailable'`, with the arm they were assigned.

Count these runs in the assigned arm. If you leave them out, an arm that fails only for some subjects looks better than it is.

## Forced assignment

Statespace does not force a subject into an arm, and clients cannot choose one. The proxy removes each `x-statespace-*` header that a client sends. Keep manual overrides out of a randomized comparison.

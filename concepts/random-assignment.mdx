---
title: Random assignment
description: Keep each subject in one arm, and each experiment's subjects apart.
---

Random assignment gives each eligible subject one arm: an arm of a branch, or a control. Each experiment version records its arms, their commits, their shares, and their allocation ranges.

## Stable identity

Choose an identifier at the level where treatment can vary independently. The `subject` rule in `statespace.toml` is a CEL expression that reads it from the request.

```toml
subject = 'request.headers["x-user-id"]'
```

Without a `subject`, the subject is `request.cookies.ssp_sid`. `ssp proxy` sets that cookie for every new visitor, so a browser keeps its arm. A request whose rule yields no subject gets the default and is not recorded.

All arms of an experiment use the same subject rule. A commit with another rule does not run in that experiment.

## Stable bucket

`ssp proxy` calculates a bucket from the app's salt and the subject ID.

```text
hash   = SHA-256(salt + ":" + subject_id)
bucket = first 53 bits of hash / 2^53
```

The same subject, salt, and allocation ranges produce the same arm on every proxy and every request.

## Arm allocation

Each arm and control holds ranges of the app's buckets. The size of the ranges is the arm's share. Running experiments of an app never share a subject, because their ranges never overlap. A subject outside every range gets the default and belongs to no experiment.

For an arm and its control at 10% each, the allocation can look like this.

```text
recent-clicks: [0.0, 0.1)
control:       [0.1, 0.2)
default:       every other bucket
```

The app's traffic limit caps the total. An arm that does not fit waits, first come, first served.

## New versions

An arm keeps its ranges across versions. A new commit of the arm, or a new arm in the experiment, starts a new version, and the subjects of the other arms stay where they are. A control of a new base takes over the ranges of a control that went. When an arm's share grows, it takes free ranges; when it shrinks, it keeps the start of its ranges.

Share changes can still move subjects between arms. Analyze each experiment version separately. Starting a new version stops assignments to the previous one.

## Unavailable arms

An arm that is starting, failing, or over a proxy's `--max-arms` cannot serve its subjects. They get the default, and their runs record `reason = 'arm-unavailable'` with the arm they were assigned. Count them in the arm they were assigned. Excluding them biases the comparison when an arm fails only for some subjects.

## Forced assignment

Statespace does not force a subject into an arm. Clients cannot choose one: the proxy removes any `x-statespace-*` header a client sends. Keep manual overrides out of a randomized comparison.

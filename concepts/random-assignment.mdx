---
title: Random assignment
description: Keep each subject in one variant, and each experiment's subjects apart.
---

Random assignment gives each eligible subject one arm: control or a variant. Each published experiment version records its arms, their allocation ranges, and its share of the app's subjects.

## Stable identity

Choose an identifier at the level where treatment can vary independently. The experiment's subject rule is a CEL expression that reads it from the request.

```shell
ssp experiment create rank-v2 --app atlas --split atlas-search=0.05 \
  --subject 'request.headers["x-user-id"]'
```

Without `--subject`, the subject is `request.cookies.ssp_sid`. `ssp proxy` sets that cookie for every new visitor, so a browser keeps its arm. A request whose rule yields no subject gets the default and is not recorded.

The subject rule cannot change after the experiment starts. Changing it would mix two populations.

## Stable bucket

`ssp proxy` calculates a bucket from a salt and the subject ID.

```text
bucket = first 53 bits of SHA-256(salt + ":" + subject_id) / 2^53
```

The same subject, salt, and allocation ranges produce the same arm on every proxy and every request.

## Slices

Running experiments of an app never share a subject. Each running experiment holds a slice of the app's buckets, calculated with the app's salt. The slice's size is the sum of its arms' shares.

```shell
ssp experiment create rank-v2 --app atlas --split atlas-search=0.1
```

This experiment holds 20% of the app's subjects: 10% for `atlas-search` and 10% for control. A subject outside every slice gets the default and belongs to no experiment. `ssp experiment start` fails when the app's free share is smaller than the slice. A restarted experiment keeps its previous slice when it is still free.

## Arm allocation

Within its slice, an experiment calculates a second bucket with its own salt. Each variant has explicit ranges of it. The remaining interval belongs to control. For an experiment whose variant and control have equal shares, the allocation is shown below.

```text
atlas-search: [0.0, 0.5)
control:      [0.5, 1.0)
```

## New versions

An experiment keeps one salt for its lifetime. Starting a changed draft keeps each variant's existing ranges where its new weight permits. New or expanded variants use free ranges. Publishing a new version of a variant keeps its ranges, so its subjects stay in the variant.

Weight changes can still move subjects between variants. Analyze each experiment version separately. Starting a new version stops assignments to the previous one.

## Unavailable arms

An arm that is starting, failing, or over a proxy's `--max-variants` cannot serve its subjects. They get the default, and their runs record `reason = 'variant-unavailable'` with the arm they were assigned. Count them in the arm they were assigned. Excluding them biases the comparison when an arm fails only for some subjects.

## Forced assignment

Statespace does not force a subject into an arm. Clients cannot choose one: the proxy removes any `x-statespace-*` header a client sends. Keep manual overrides out of a randomized comparison.

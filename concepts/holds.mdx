title: Holds
description: How stop and resume work for an app and for one arm.
---

How do you take an arm out of traffic at once, and bring it back?

A stop places a **hold**. Statespace keeps the hold until someone resumes it. The arm leaves traffic in seconds, and its commit status becomes `failure`.

```shell
ssp app stop shop-prod --note "checkout errors > 2%"
ssp arm stop a1b2c3d --app shop-prod
```

## Two levels

| Hold | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| On the app | Every arm of the app, now and added later |
| On an arm | That arm only |

## Rules

- A stop on a target that has a hold returns the hold. It changes nothing.
- Resume clears only the hold that it names. An arm can have a hold of its own and a hold on its app. Then `ssp app resume` keeps the arm's hold, and says so.
- Resume never brings back a removed arm.
- Stop and resume never ask for confirmation. So agents and scripts can run them.
- A hold starts a new version of each arm that it covers. Its end starts one too.
- Statespace keeps roles for 10 minutes. So stop and resume work while GitHub is down.

## What a hold records

Each hold records:

- the actor, such as `maria`;
- the kind of actor: `person`, `workflow`, or `token`;
- where it came from: `cli`, `dashboard`, or `api`;
- the note;
- the time.

The commit status says who stopped the arm and how. Then it gives the note.

```text
Stopped by maria with ssp arm stop · checkout errors > 2%
```
